# Questions for clinician review

100 questions, drafted from standard anatomy references
(Gray's, Moore, Last's) without a clinician. Please check each stem, answer and
explanation before launch. The first 60 items were approved by the clinician on 2026-10-13. The 40 organ and
portal-system items added afterwards are marked draft and still need review.

Answers are structures in the 3D model of the trunk (thorax, back, abdominal wall
and pelvis). The model is BodyParts3D 3.0 (DBCLS; CC BY 4.0); the heart chambers,
main bronchi, colon segments, bile ducts, portal and hepatic veins and testicular
vessels come from BodyParts3D 4.0 (CC BY 4.0, via the Human Atlas repository) and
were shifted onto the 3.0 skeleton, so they can sit up to about 1.5 cm off.
Schematic nerve and vessel tubes were added because BodyParts3D has no trunk
nerves, spinal cord, azygos or internal thoracic vessels. When the
schematic structures are in use, their courses are listed at the end for review
too. Spelling is UK.

## Schematic nerve and vessel courses

BodyParts3D 3.0 has no trunk nerves, no spinal cord and no azygos, thoracic duct,
internal thoracic, epigastric or intercostal vessels, so these 33 structures are
tubes built by `tools/trunk_nerves.py`. Their control points are derived in code
from this model's bones (spinal canal and foramina found by rays through each
vertebra, rib heads and the inferior border of each rib, iliac crest, anterior
superior iliac spine, pubic tubercle, ischial spine and tuberosity, obturator
foramen) and from the sections and surfaces of its muscles, organs and vessels;
then every path is pushed clear of the bones. Paired structures are drawn on the
right only. Diameters are typical values, branches are mostly left out, and each
nerve is one tube from its origin to where it ends in this model. Please check:

- **Spinal cord**: centre of the vertebral canal at each thoracic level (midway
  between the back of the body and the front of the lamina), with a short
  cervical lead-in above T1, ending as the conus at the L1/L2 disc. Diameter 10 mm
  throughout (no cervical or lumbar enlargement); no meninges or nerve roots
  other than those listed below.
- **Cauda equina**: one tube for the whole bundle in the lumbar canal, from the
  conus to the top of the sacral canal. The dural sac really ends at S2; the
  simplified sacrum mesh has no open sacral canal, so the tube stops at S1.
- **Phrenic nerve** (right): from behind the internal jugular vein, between the
  subclavian artery and vein, down the right side of the superior vena cava, over
  the pericardium of the right atrium (right border of the heart), in front of the
  lung root, to the diaphragm beside the inferior vena cava, where it ends just
  under the upper surface. The cervical course on scalenus anterior is not drawn
  (no scalene muscles in the model).
- **Vagus nerve** (right, thoracic course): in the carotid sheath between the
  internal jugular vein and common carotid artery, in front of the first part of
  the subclavian artery, down the right side of the trachea, behind the right
  main bronchus (posterior to the lung root), onto the back of the esophagus,
  through the esophageal hiatus to the back of the stomach near the cardia. The
  recurrent laryngeal loop, the pulmonary and esophageal plexuses and the left
  vagus are not drawn.
- **Sympathetic trunk** (right): over the front of the rib heads (costovertebral
  joints) from the first rib to the twelfth, then on the anterolateral lumbar
  bodies medial to psoas, then on the front of the sacrum medial to the anterior
  sacral foramina, ending near the midline at the lower sacrum (ganglion impar).
  Ganglia, rami communicantes and the splanchnic nerves are not drawn.
- **Fourth intercostal nerve** (right): from the canal through the T4/T5
  intervertebral foramen (found as the widest gap between the two vertebrae and
  the rib heads), then just below the inferior border of rib 4 (the costal
  groove) from beyond the rib neck to the anterior end of the rib, along the
  fourth costal cartilage to the side of the sternum, and forward as the anterior
  cutaneous branch. The lateral cutaneous branch is not drawn.
- **Seventh intercostal nerve** (right): through the T7/T8 foramen, along the
  inferior border of rib 7 to its anterior end at the costal margin, then up and
  medially behind the costal margin into the rectus sheath just below the
  xiphoid, ending in rectus abdominis. The lateral cutaneous branch is not drawn.
- **Tenth intercostal nerve** (right): through the T10/T11 foramen, along the
  inferior border of rib 10 to its anterior end, then between transversus
  abdominis and internal oblique round the flank into the rectus sheath at the
  level of the umbilicus (taken as the L3/L4 disc level), ending in rectus
  abdominis. The lateral cutaneous branch is not drawn.
- **Subcostal nerve (T12)** (right): through the T12/L1 foramen, along the
  inferior border of rib 12, then between transversus abdominis and internal
  oblique round the flank to the rectus sheath about 3 cm below the umbilicus.
  Its course in front of quadratus lumborum and behind the kidney is only implied.
- **Long thoracic nerve** (right): from behind the clavicle down the lateral
  surface of serratus anterior in the mid-axillary line to about the eighth rib.
  Its roots (C5 to C7) and the course through scalenus medius are not drawn.
- **Thoracodorsal nerve** (right): from the posterior wall of the axilla along
  the front of the lateral border of the scapula to the deep surface of
  latissimus dorsi near its anterior border, below the inferior angle.
- **Dorsal scapular nerve** (right): from deep (anterior) to levator scapulae in
  the neck to the superior angle of the scapula, then down along the medial
  border of the scapula on the deep surface of the rhomboids.
- **Spinal accessory nerve** (right, CN XI): enters under the anterior border of
  trapezius in the neck, then descends on the deep surface of trapezius about
  halfway between the spinous processes and the medial border of the scapula, to
  below the inferior angle. Its course in the posterior triangle and through
  sternocleidomastoid is not drawn.
- **Iliohypogastric nerve** (right): from the lateral border of psoas at L1,
  obliquely across the front of quadratus lumborum, through transversus abdominis
  above the iliac crest, forward between transversus and internal oblique, through
  internal oblique medial to the anterior superior iliac spine, ending deep to
  the external oblique aponeurosis about 3 to 4 cm above the superficial ring.
- **Ilioinguinal nerve** (right): parallel to and just below the iliohypogastric
  nerve to the anterior superior iliac spine, then into the inguinal canal above
  the medial half of the inguinal ligament (in front of the deep ring), out
  through the superficial ring above the pubic tubercle and a short way toward
  the scrotum or labium. The deep and superficial rings are not modelled; they
  are placed from the inguinal ligament (deep ring 1.5 cm above its midpoint,
  superficial ring above and lateral to its medial end).
- **Genitofemoral nerve** (right): emerges on the front of psoas at about L3,
  runs down its anterior surface, then lateral to the external iliac artery to the
  deep inguinal ring (genital branch). The femoral branch under the ligament is
  not drawn.
- **Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve** (right): from the lateral border of psoas at
  L4, obliquely across iliacus to pass under the inguinal ligament about 1.5 cm
  medial to the anterior superior iliac spine, ending 3.5 cm into the thigh (the
  model has no thigh muscles).
- **Femoral nerve** (right): from the lateral border of psoas, in the groove
  between psoas and iliacus, under the inguinal ligament lateral to the femoral
  artery, ending 3.5 cm below the ligament where it would divide. Branches are
  not drawn.
- **Obturator nerve** (right): from the medial border of psoas at the pelvic brim
  along the lateral pelvic wall (on obturator internus) to the obturator canal
  at the upper edge of the obturator foramen, and just out of it.
- **Lumbosacral trunk** (right): from the L4 ventral ramus at the medial edge of
  psoas, over the front of the sacral ala, down in front of the sacroiliac joint to
  the front of piriformis, where it joins S1 to form the sciatic nerve.
- **Sciatic nerve** (right): from its formation in front of piriformis, out of the
  greater sciatic foramen below piriformis, then down behind the ischium (on
  obturator internus) between the ischial tuberosity and the hip joint, ending
  below the hip bone deep to gluteus maximus. The model has no femur, gemelli or
  quadratus femoris, so the level below the tuberosity is approximate.
- **Pudendal nerve** (right): from in front of piriformis and coccygeus (S2 to
  S4, drawn as a single start next to the sciatic origin), out of the greater
  sciatic foramen below piriformis and medial to the sciatic nerve, round the back
  of the ischial spine, in through the lesser sciatic foramen and forward in the
  pudendal canal on the medial surface of obturator internus, ending toward the
  perineum. Its branches are not drawn.
- **L4 nerve root** (right): leaves the cauda equina at L4, runs out through the
  L4/L5 intervertebral foramen (found as the widest gap between the two
  vertebrae) and ends as the L4 ventral ramus at the medial edge of psoas, where
  the lumbosacral trunk begins. Its contributions to the femoral and obturator
  nerves inside psoas are not drawn.
- **L5 nerve root** (right): leaves the cauda equina at L5, out through the L5/S1
  foramen, and joins the lumbosacral trunk over the sacral ala.
- **S1 nerve root** (right): leaves the cauda equina at the top of the sacral
  canal, crosses to the first anterior sacral foramen and joins the lumbosacral
  trunk at the sciatic origin in front of piriformis. The simplified sacrum mesh
  has no open sacral canal or first sacral foramen, so the first 2 to 3 cm of
  this root lie inside the sacrum by design.
- **Internal thoracic artery** (right): from the underside of the first part of
  the subclavian artery, down about 1 cm lateral to the sternal border on the back
  of the costal cartilages (in front of transversus thoracis), ending at the sixth
  intercostal space, where it divides.
- **Seventh posterior intercostal artery** (right): from the right side of the
  descending aorta across the front of the T7 body (behind the esophagus), into
  the seventh intercostal space and along the costal groove of rib 7 just above
  the seventh intercostal nerve, ending before the anterior end of the rib.
- **Azygos vein**: on the right front of the vertebral bodies from L2 up to T5,
  then arching forward over the right main bronchus at T4 into the back of the
  superior vena cava. Its tributaries (hemiazygos, intercostal veins) are not
  drawn.
- **Superior epigastric artery** (right): continues the internal thoracic artery
  from the sixth intercostal space behind the costal margin into the rectus
  sheath, down the back of rectus abdominis to the level of the umbilicus.
- **Inferior epigastric artery** (right): from the external iliac artery just above
  the inguinal ligament, up medial to the deep inguinal ring (the lateral border of
  Hesselbach's triangle), onto the back of rectus abdominis, to the umbilicus.
- **Thoracic duct**: from a cisterna chyli level at L1/L2 on the front of the
  vertebral bodies, up through the aortic hiatus between the aorta and the azygos
  vein behind the esophagus, crossing to the left at T5 to T4, up on the left of
  the esophagus, arching above T1 and ending at the left venous angle. The model
  has no left internal jugular or subclavian vein, so the left venous angle is the
  mirror image of the right one. The cisterna chyli is not drawn as a sac.
- **Brachiocephalic trunk**: from the top of the right part of the aortic arch up
  and to the right to where the right subclavian and right common carotid
  arteries begin in this model (behind the right sternoclavicular joint).
- **Femoral artery (below the inguinal ligament)**: a 4 cm continuation of the
  lower end of the external iliac artery mesh into the femoral triangle, so that
  the inguinal ligament and the vessels can be told apart for the inguinal versus
  femoral hernia question. No femoral vein, canal or ring is drawn.
